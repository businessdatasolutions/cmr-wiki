MOST DECISIONS AREN’T FINAL — SOMETIMES, YOU JUST NEED THE COURAGE TO WALK
THROUGH THE DOOR.


Jeff Bezos’ Method of Decision Making: The
Two-Way Door Strategy
How a billionaire’s strategy can simplify decision-making and boost productivity

For anyone overwhelmed by the weight of daily high-stakes decisions, stuck in a
cycle of overthinking and inaction, Jeff Bezos offers a simple yet effective solution:
the two-way door strategy. He states that decision are of two kinds, the two-way door
decision, a decision you can easily crawl back from; and one-way door decision, a
decision which is very difficult to reverse.

Two-Way Door Decisions: Embrace Speed and Flexibility

In a podcast with Lex Fridman, Jeff Bezos revealed his method of how he makes
innumerable decisions daily for his vast businesses. Jeff says:

“Most decisions are two-way doors. If you make the wrong decision, if it’s a two-way door
you pick a door you walk out you spend a little time there, if it turns out to be the wrong
decision you can come back in and pick another door.”

As a bussiness owner, or anyone in a position who has to make high-stakes decisions
on a daily basis, we often start to overthink all sorts of decisions without taking a
step-back to firstly analyse the weight and consequence of the decision. This is a
trait especially common in perfectionists, who simply want everything done
according to their exact vision and have a hard time in sharing control. However,
such an approach leads to lack of pace and inability to scale your organisation, as
efficient institutions require speed as well as precision. Hence, it is very important
for business owners to categorise each decision and then allocate their time finding
its conclusion.

To identify two-way doors, you need to identify decisions that are reversible and
carry the least risk. Think of testing a new feature on your website, piloting a
different advertising strategy, or experimenting a pilot run with a new supplier.
Even if things don’t go as planned, you could always start over without significant
loss.

Most business owners overthink these decisions, wasting valuable time and
resources. Bezos’s framework encourages agility: make the call, act quickly, and
adjust based on outcomes. This approach fosters experimentation and innovation
without the paralysis of excessive deliberation.

A classic example is of this renowned businessman who thought of teaching his
learnings to an audience online. He launched a bussiness learning course online but
a month later, there were no buyers! However, the businessman made no loss of
major time or money. How is it that you ask? He simply launched the course but
never really made it! The businessman decided to launch the course and then
actually make it if there were any buyers.

One-Way Door Decisions: Proceed with Caution

On the other hand, one-way door decisions are permanent or extremely hard to
reverse. Bezos stated in the same podcast mentioned earlier:

“Some decisions are so consequential and so important and so hard to reverse that they
really are one-way door decisions. You go in that door you’re not coming back and those
decisions have to be made very deliberately very carefully. If you can think of yet another
way to analyze the decision you should slow down and do that.”

These might include acquiring another business, making a significant branding
change, or entering a legally binding long-term contract. Because these choices can
reshape the future of your business, they demand careful thought and a structured
decision-making process.

Bezos advises that for these decisions, you should slow down, seek input from
trusted advisors, and analyze all potential outcomes. For example, a clothing-
manufacturer wanting to expand deciding whether he should acquire neighbouring
land to expand his one-story operations, or to increase floors on his current setup.
Such a decision carries significant capital, logistics and compliance considerations,
and can not be reversed without causing a significant dent to the business.

How to Apply the Framework in Your Business

 1. Decision Categorisation: Ask yourself: “If this fails, can I easily reverse it?” or
   “Do I have a qualified team who is able come uop with better solutions” If yes,
   it’s a two-way door. If no, it’s a one-way door.

 2. Pace on Two-Way Doors: Reduce the analysis for reversible decisions. Set a clear
   timeline or preferably, delegate the responsibility to teams within your
   organisation and make the call. Focus on learning rather than perfection.

 3. Steady for One-Way Doors: Spend time gathering data, consulting stakeholders,
   and analysing risks for irreversible decisions. Be thorough, but don’t let fear
   paralyse you.
 4. Reflect and Iterate: Regardless of the decision type, review its outcome. What
    worked? What didn’t? Use these insights to refine your future choices.

The Takeaway: A Balanced Approach

The beauty of the two-way door framework lies in its simplicity. It encourages
business owners to allocate their energy wisely, embracing speed and flexibility for
low-stakes decisions while reserving time and deliberation for those with far-
reaching consequences.

Such a strategy also helps you reflect key areas of focus within your organisation
and help you understand where your energies are required to lead to optimal
growth. Moreover, upon reflection and clear categorisation you will understand you
will better understand what tasks need to delegated and where to add people to
increase organisational efficiency. Such an approach is also supported by Timothy
Ferriss’ “The 4-Hour Workweek”, which states “80% of the consequences flow from
20% of the causes. 80% of the results come from 20% of the effort and time.”

By incorporating this approach into your decision-making process, you’ll strike a
balance between agility and caution, empowering your business to adapt, learn, and
grow. After all, most decisions in life and business aren’t as final as they seem —
sometimes, all you need is the courage to walk through the door.
