# The 10 elements of culture that matter most to employees

> The ranked drivers of how employees rate their company's culture, from a SHAP analysis of 1.4M Glassdoor reviews across 150+ topics ([[measuring-culture-with-nlp|CultureX NLP]]). Respect is the single strongest predictor — ~18× the average topic. Reproduction of the named list + the SHAP importance chart from Sull & Sull (2021).

## The 10 elements (ranked by importance)

| # | Element | Theme | What it captures |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Employees feel respected** | Respect | Treated with consideration, courtesy, dignity; perspectives taken seriously. **~18× the average topic; ~2× the #2 factor — the single best predictor.** |
| 2 | **Supportive leaders** | Leadership | Leaders help employees, respond to requests, accommodate needs, encourage, "have their backs." |
| 3 | **Leaders live core values** | Leadership | Leaders' actions are consistent with stated values ("walk the talk"). |
| 4 | **Toxic managers** | Leadership | Leaders create a poisonous environment (abusive, disrespectful, non-inclusive, unethical). |
| 5 | **Unethical behaviour** | Leadership | Managers/employees lack integrity, act unethically. |
| 6 | **Benefits** | Comp & benefits | Assessment of employer-provided benefits (**2× more predictive than compensation**). |
| 7 | **Perks** | Comp & benefits | Workplace amenities; company-organized social events are an especially strong predictor. |
| 8 | **Learning and development** | Growth | Formal and informal learning opportunities. |
| 9 | **Job security** | Security & change | Perceived security; fear of layoffs, offshoring, automation. |
| 10 | **Reorganizations** | Security & change | View of reorgs (discussed negatively **97%** of the time; signals strategic churn). |

## The counter-intuitive part

Topics leaders *expect* to matter — **friendly colleagues, flexible schedules, manageable workloads** — were commonly discussed but had **little or no impact** on a firm's culture score. And **compensation** matters far less than benefits for culture rating ("compensation matters, but it won't fix a broken culture").

## Critical distinction: this list ≠ the Big 9

These are the drivers of **how employees rate culture** (the employee-experience dependent variable). They are **not** the [[big-9-cultural-values|Big 9]] (the values *companies cite*), and **not** the [[cultural-archetypes|archetypes that predict firm financials]] (where performance-orientation dominates and people-culture shows no objective effect). Three lists, three different questions:

| List | Question it answers | Top item |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [[big-9-cultural-values\|Big 9]] | What do firms *aspire to*? | (most-cited official values) |
| **10 elements (this artifact)** | What predicts *employee culture ratings*? | **Respect (~18×)** |
| [[cultural-archetypes\|Archetypes]] | What predicts *objective firm performance*? | **Performance-orientation** |

## How to use this artifact

- **As the employee-experience benchmark** for culture interventions — respect, supportive/values-living leaders, and rooting out toxic/unethical managers are the highest-leverage moves.
- **Paired with [[oreilly-2024-archetype-performance-regressions]]** to avoid conflating "what employees value" with "what drives financials."
