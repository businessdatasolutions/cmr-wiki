## Why this exhibit is load-bearing

Porter uses ROIC (not return on sales or profit growth) because it is the appropriate profitability measure for strategy — it controls for the capital required to compete and for idiosyncratic differences in capital structure and tax rates. The headline observation: **soft drinks and prepackaged software are ~6× more profitable than airlines** over the period. Since these industries differ on almost every visible attribute (high-tech vs low-tech, product vs service, emerging vs mature), the persistent spread cannot be explained by those attributes — it is explained by **industry structure** as captured by the five forces. This is the empirical anchor for the framework's central claim.

## How to use this artifact

- **As evidence** that structure drives profitability — the motivating data behind [[porter-five-forces]].
- **As a benchmarking reference** for the rough profitability ceiling of structurally similar industries (with the caveat that the data is U.S., 1992–2006, and structure shifts over time).
- **As a teaching exhibit** pairing the qualitative framework with a quantitative payoff.
