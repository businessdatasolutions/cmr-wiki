# Damodaran's 10 principles of risk management

> Verbatim reproduction of the 10 boxed principle statements from Aswath Damodaran's *Strategic Risk Taking: A Framework for Risk Management* (Wharton School Publishing, 2008), Chapter 12 — *Risk Management: First Principles*. ## The principles

### Risk Management 1 — The unexpected dominates

> *Your biggest risks will come from places that you least expect them to come from and in forms that you least expected them to take. The essence of good risk management is to be able to roll with the punches, when confronted with the unexpected.*

**Context** — opens with the "risk is everywhere" framing and the three shifts in the risk landscape (globalisation, cross-sector spillover, financial-market risk emergence). Worked illustrations: the Feb 27, 2007 Shanghai/Dow correlation event; the sub-prime/Yahoo cross-sector example.

### Risk Management 2 — Balance, not minimisation

> *Risk is a mix of upside and downside. Good risk management is not about seeking out or avoiding risk, but about maintaining the right balance between the two.*

**Context** — the upside reframe that distinguishes Damodaran's strategic-risk-taking lens from traditional finance treatment. Anchors the wiki's [[strategic-risk-management]] concept.

### Risk Management 3 — Human endeavour

> *Managing risk is a human endeavor and a risk management system is only as good as the people manning it.*

**Context** — recognises behavioural-finance findings that risk attitudes are unstable (loss aversion, framing effects, gambler's-fallacy risk-seeking after losses).

### Risk Management 4 — Perspective consistency

> *To manage risk right, you have to pick the right perspective on risk and stay consistent through the process to that perspective. In other words, if you choose to view risk through the eyes of investors in the firm, you will assess and behave accordingly.*

**Context** — same risk reads very differently from line-manager vs top-manager vs investor perspective. The principle is not about which perspective is correct but about *not switching* mid-process. Accompanies the chapter's 5-axis risk-categorisation taxonomy.

### Risk Management 5 — Tool understanding

> *To pick the right tool to assess risk, you have to understand what the tools share in common, what they do differently and how to use the output from each tool.*

**Context** — the toolkit (risk-adjusted discount rates, simulations, scenario analysis, VaR, decision trees) has expanded and become more accessible; tool selection is now a critical management discipline.

### Risk Management 6 — Tools serve decisions

> *The tools to assess risk and the output from risk assessment should be tailored to the decision-making process, rather than the other way around.*

**Context** — risk-assessment products often fail because assessors and decision-makers sit in different parts of the org. The principle inverts the usual flow: tool design starts from what decisions need to be made.

### Risk Management 7 — Avoid / hedge / exploit

> *Hedging risk is but a small part of risk management. Determining which risks should be hedged, which should not and which should be taken advantage is the key to successful risk management.*

**Context** — the three-bucket allocation (avoid / pass-through / exploit) at the operational heart of SRM. Worked example: Boeing's contract-specific FX exposure as a case where firm-level hedging beats investor-level diversification.

### Risk Management 8 — Value as the metric

> *To manage risk right, you have to understand the levers that determine the value of a business.*

**Context** — the only metric that matters for judging risk management is its impact on business value. Stock-price-targeting and earnings-volatility-targeting both produce distorted decisions. Requires richer valuation models than the discount-rate-only models in common use.

### Risk Management 9 — Everyone's job

> *Managing risk well is the essence of good business practice and is everyone's responsibility.*

**Context** — pushes back against the side-effect of having a dedicated risk-management function: that other functions stop thinking about risk. Operations, marketing, advertising, R&D all carry risk-management components of their decisions.

### Risk Management 10 — Structure and culture

> *To succeed at risk management, you have to embed it in the organization through its structure and culture and get the right people.*

**Context** — anchors the **five ingredients of the successful risk-taking organisation**: (a) alignment of interests via compensation and governance, (b) good and timely information judged by crisis-mode performance, (c) solid analysis built on tool understanding, (d) flexibility correlated with flat structure / smaller size / less vested in existing tech, (e) people who respond well to risk, retained with financial rewards + culture + team dynamics.

## How to use this artifact

- **As a diagnostic checklist** for an existing risk-management programme — score the programme against each of the 10 principles.
- **As the entry curriculum** for an SRM training programme — each principle is a half-hour briefing seed.
- **As citation backbone** when a wiki page argues a position on risk management — link directly to the relevant principle on this page rather than re-quoting Damodaran.
