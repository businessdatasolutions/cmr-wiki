# Turning an SME into an Innovation Powerhouse — Strategyzer webinar (Osterwalder & Viki 2024)

> In this interactive live session with Alex Osterwalder and Tendayi Viki, the spotlight was on the advantages small and mid-sized enterprises (SMEs) have in fostering innovation … Learn how Alex and Tendayi dispelled three common myths about innovation.

(Strategyzer's framing of the webinar.)

## TL;DR

A Strategyzer webinar write-up (Alex Osterwalder & Tendayi Viki, 29 Nov 2024) dispelling **three innovation myths**: (1) *innovation needs big investment* — false (Quibi burned $1.75B and failed; Laurastar succeeded with parallel low-cost exploration); (2) *innovation needs R&D/technology* — false (PwC's *Innovation 1000* found ~zero correlation between R&D spend and returns over ~two decades; value comes from **business-model innovation**, not technology); (3) *innovation is harder in SMEs* — false (SMEs' flat structure, decision-maker proximity, and customer closeness are **superpowers** for rapid, cheap experimentation). The throughline: **business-model innovation** — finding the right model to create, deliver and monetise value — beats product/technology novelty, and SMEs are structurally advantaged at it.

## Results (WHAT) — three myths debunked

### Myth 1 — "SMEs need a lot of investment"

False. Substantial investment is *not* a key driver of success. **Quibi** raised $1.75B with a seasoned eBay/Disney leadership team to disrupt Netflix with mobile micro-content — and went from huge funding to irrelevance fast. The mistake: it **didn't distinguish Explore from Exploit**. "There are no winners in innovation. What you do is create the conditions for winners to emerge." Contrast **Laurastar** (Swiss high-end steam irons): it invested in **three teams exploring value propositions and business models in parallel**, tested three models, and landed on *Iggy* (a handheld steamer that also purifies fabrics). The key was a **business-model shift**, not a big budget.

### Myth 2 — "Innovation requires R&D or technology"

False. Tendayi Viki cited **PwC's Innovation 1000** reports showing a **consistent ~zero correlation between R&D investment and returns over ~two decades**. Two firms investing the same can see +50% vs −50% outcomes. "Having a cool technology is not the same as having a value proposition." Innovation is about **creating value for customers and the company through strategic business-model innovation** — desirability + viability + the model to monetise. IP protection has its place but the emphasis should be on delivering customer value. For SMEs, business-model innovation isn't about groundbreaking inventions — it's **finding the right business model** for their product/service.

> "Business model innovation is the biggest unexplored possibility for large and small companies because very few people master it." — Alex Osterwalder

### Myth 3 — "Innovation is harder in SMEs"

False. SMEs can move with more **speed and agility** by tapping their "superpowers":

- **Flatter structure / less hierarchy**, greater access to decision-makers.
- **Decision-maker = economic buyer = influencer** are often the same person — less buy-in friction for business-model innovation.
- **Easier to run a rapid-experimentation tech stack** (large firms' vendor onboarding / 90-day payment terms make hiring designers/developers for experiments hard).
- **Customer closeness** (especially B2B) → faster learning and testing.

The ability to move quickly and cheaply, and to show evidence to support/refute an idea fast, is a decisive advantage in resource-constrained environments.
