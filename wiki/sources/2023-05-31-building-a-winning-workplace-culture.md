# Building a Winning Workplace Culture — MIT SMR webinar (Don & Charlie Sull, 2023)

> Building a strong, healthy culture is a management imperative in today's workforce. Researchers Donald Sull and Charlie Sull found that toxic culture was the single best predictor of attrition during the first six months of the Great Resignation — 10 times more powerful than compensation. To get culture right, managers need to know first how to measure it, and second how to analyze results and address problems. This webinar will unlock actionable insights for tackling toxic culture and building a winning workplace.

(MIT SMR's description of the webinar.)

## Context (WHY)

Set in the aftermath of the **Great Resignation** and a wave of tech layoffs, the webinar's premise is that culture is now a board-level financial concern, not an HR soft topic — and that leaders fail at it because they cannot *measure* it. It is the practitioner-facing companion to the Sulls' published research ([[2021-09-16-ten-things-corporate-culture]] and the [[2024-06-20-oreilly-2024-culture-archetypes-firm-performance|culture-archetypes study]]).

## Results (WHAT) — key claims

- **Culture → firm value.** Wharton survey: >90% of CEOs/CFOs say good culture increases firm value; a near-identical share say toxic culture leads to unethical behaviour/fraud. Notably surveyed CEOs/CFOs, not CHROs.
- **Definition.** Culture = values *deeply held, widely shared, and shaping behaviour day-to-day*.
- **Official values are unreliable.** ~80% of ~700 large firms publish 3–5 core values, but a separate study found **~zero correlation** between the values a firm emphasises and how well employees say it lives them. Wells Fargo's pre-fraud values are the cautionary anecdote.
- **Measure from employee voice.** Glassdoor reviews + NLP ([[measuring-culture-with-nlp]]) give a scalable, behaviour-grounded measure. (The webinar also rebuts the "only disgruntled people review" objection — Glassdoor distributions are less extreme than Yelp's, and reviews include *departed* employees who reveal what's broken.)
- **Toxic culture is the #1 attrition driver** in the early Great Resignation — 10× more powerful than compensation.
- **Acting on culture.** An employee-involvement programme (intact teams of 10–15 meeting weekly for ~6 months) translates an overarching value like *respect* into **context-specific social norms** (norms of civility differ between a rural Alabama hospital and a NYC hospital). Selection, promotion, and exits are as powerful as hiring for shaping culture; culture involves **trade-offs** ("anybody who tells you you can have it all…").
