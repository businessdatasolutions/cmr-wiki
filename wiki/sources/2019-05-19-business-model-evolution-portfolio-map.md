# Business Model Evolution Using the Portfolio Map — Strategyzer (Osterwalder & Pigneur 2019)

> We have been developing our Business Model Portfolio Map for our new book, *The Invincible Company*. Like all of our tools, we have found that the Portfolio Map is a great storytelling tool. It can be used to show the evolution of a company's business model over its lifecycle by measuring risk vs return.

(Strategyzer's framing.)

## TL;DR

Strategyzer's **Business Model Portfolio Map** plots a company's business activities on a **risk-vs-return** field split into an **Explore portfolio** (search/iterative innovation) and an **Exploit portfolio** (existing business). The article maps a company's lifecycle by combining **Clay Christensen's business-model lifecycle** with the Portfolio Map: four **Explore stages** (Ideation → Discovery → Validation → Confirmation), a **Transfer** stage that hands an innovation from Explore to Exploit, and six **Exploit stages** (Transfer/Launch → Sustaining Innovation → Efficiency → Mature → Declining → Renovation). **Hilti** (power-tool manufacturer) is the worked case: founded 1941, scaled to a global manufacturer, and — facing the 2013 financial crisis and low-cost competition — **reframed from product manufacturer to service provider ("tools as a service")**, lifting 2018 net sales to CHF 5.7B.

A companion primary source for the [[business-model-innovation]] concept page.

## Results (WHAT) — the lifecycle on the Portfolio Map

### Explore portfolio — four stages (search / iterative)

1. **Ideation** — new ideas for value propositions and business models around an opportunity, technology, product or service.
2. **Discovery** — first evidence of desirability and viability; light sprints to test "is there something here?".
3. **Validation** — strong evidence of desirability/viability + first feasibility; build a technical MVP — "can we build this?".
4. **Confirmation** — proceed or pivot; invest in customer acquisition, marketing, robust MVPs — "can we do this at scale?".

**Transfer** — the shift from innovation to implementation; the hand-off from the Explore portfolio to the Exploit portfolio.

### Exploit portfolio — six stages (existing business)

1. **Transfer / Launch** — launch a compelling value proposition & model (customer focus).
2. **Sustaining Innovation** — scale to meet demand (market/scale focus).
3. **Efficiency** — generate profitability via cost reduction / productivity / redesign (shareholder focus).
4. **Mature Business** — established, loyal base, competitive field, stable growth.
5. **Declining Business** — declining profitability, obsolescence, shrinking base.
6. **Renovation** — before the business dies, shift the model toward a new product/service/model to survive and grow.

### Worked case — Hilti

Family business (1941) → global power-tool manufacturer → culture/quality revamp → 2010 net sales CHF 4B → 2013 financial crisis + low-cost competition → **reframe from product manufacturer to service provider ("tools as a service")** → 2018 net sales CHF 5.7B. A canonical **Renovation** (business-model renewal) story.

## Discussion / Significance (SO WHAT)

1. **It is the *tool* that operationalises the [[organizational-ambidexterity]] continuum** — where [[2020-03-16-explore-exploit-continuum]] names the spectrum, the Portfolio Map plots companies on it by risk vs return.
2. **It supplies a stage vocabulary for [[business-model-innovation]]** — the 4 Explore + Transfer + 6 Exploit stages give a usable lifecycle map.
3. **Hilti is a reusable Renovation exemplar** — a clean product→service business-model shift that connects to servitization and the W&W business-model-renewal outcome.

**Limitations:** short practitioner blog; the framework is proprietary; Christensen's "lifecycle" is invoked loosely; Hilti figures are reported, not audited.
