# Jeff Bezos' Two-Way Door Decision-Making — Medium (Yoshida 2025)

> Most decisions aren't final — sometimes, you just need the courage to walk through the door.

(The article's epigraph; Dani Yoshida's framing of Bezos's heuristic.)

## TL;DR

A short Medium piece popularising Jeff Bezos's **two-way door vs one-way door** decision heuristic (originally from Bezos's Amazon shareholder letters and reprised in his Lex Fridman podcast interview). **Two-way door decisions** are reversible and low-risk — make them fast, delegate them, optimise for learning and speed. **One-way door decisions** are consequential and hard/impossible to reverse — slow down, gather data, consult, deliberate. The error most leaders make is treating reversible decisions as if they were irreversible, producing analysis-paralysis that kills organisational pace. The article adds a four-step application recipe and links the idea to the Pareto (80/20) principle.

## Results (WHAT)

### Two-way door decisions — speed and flexibility

> "Most decisions are two-way doors. If you make the wrong decision … you can come back in and pick another door." — Bezos (via Lex Fridman podcast)

Reversible, low-risk decisions: testing a website feature, piloting an ad strategy, a trial run with a new supplier. Even if they fail, you can start over without significant loss. The advice: **reduce analysis, set a timeline or delegate to teams, make the call, adjust on outcomes.** This fosters experimentation and innovation without paralysis.

### One-way door decisions — caution

> "Some decisions are so consequential and so important and so hard to reverse that they really are one-way door decisions. You go in that door, you're not coming back …" — Bezos

Permanent or near-irreversible: acquiring a business, a major rebrand, a binding long-term contract. These demand deliberate, structured analysis and trusted-advisor input.

### Application recipe (four steps)

1. **Categorise:** "If this fails, can I easily reverse it?" Yes → two-way; No → one-way.
2. **Pace two-way doors:** reduce analysis, set timelines, delegate, focus on learning over perfection.
3. **Steady for one-way doors:** gather data, consult stakeholders, analyse risks — but don't let fear paralyse.
4. **Reflect and iterate:** review outcomes regardless of type; refine future choices.

The article closes by linking the heuristic to Tim Ferriss's restatement of the **Pareto principle** (80% of consequences flow from 20% of causes) — focus deliberation where irreversibility concentrates consequence.
