# Yves Morieux (BCG): Organisations Respond to Complexity with Complicatedness — Brain Bar 2023

> Today, challenges that organisations have to solve are 6 times more complex than at around the middle of the 20th century. Companies' solution to this doesn't seem too simple, either: it's more and more committees, dashboards, KPIs and endless reports. All this makes organisations 35(!) times more complicated than they were 50 years ago, with the lowest productivity growth rates of the last century in an age when AI, the cloud and other fantastic technological advances would allow us to thrive more than ever before.

(Brain Bar's description of the talk.)

## TL;DR

**Yves Morieux** (Managing Director, BCG Washington D.C.; organisational economist) argues a **productivity paradox**: business challenges are ~**6× more complex** than mid-20th-century, organisations have become ~**35× more complicated** (committees, layers, silos, KPIs, reports), and productivity growth has fallen to its lowest of the century *despite* abundant innovation (computers, big data, cloud, AI). The error: **organisations respond to complexity with complicatedness** — adding structure, process, and reporting instead of enabling **cooperation**. Reporting substitutes for trust ("I ask you to report on the action I don't trust you to have taken — why trust the report?"); the worst move is using **AI/digital to automate the internal bureaucracy** (apps to get approvals, GPT to write reports) rather than to manage complexity. The fix is **Smart Simplicity**: cooperation, engagement, and judgment (quality) that better use people's collective intelligence — the roundabout (cooperate, use intelligence) vs the traffic-light crossroads (obey markings) metaphor.

## Context (WHY)

Morieux is an organisational economist; his premise is that **productivity growth is the primary driver of living standards** — and it is stalling. Doubling living standards once took ~one generation when productivity grew well; at current rates it takes ~**six generations**. The puzzle: innovation is abundant yet productivity improvement is vanishing. His answer locates the lost productivity *inside* organisations — in the complicatedness with which they respond to a genuinely more complex world.

## Results (WHAT) — the argument

- **Complexity vs complicatedness.** Complexity is real and rising (more diversified, more sophisticated products — cf. the *economic complexity index*). **Complicatedness** is the self-inflicted organisational response: clusters, silos, functions, layers, processes, coordination bodies.
- **The light-bulb parable.** Changing a light bulb requires ~seven people/approvals to agree (facility manager, supervisor, operational buyer, category manager, safety technician, compliance manager…) — vivid complicatedness.
- **Reporting ≠ trust.** "I ask you to write a report on the action I don't trust you to have taken — why should I trust the report?" Managers spend large fractions of time in meetings and writing reports; people disengage.
- **The AI warning.** "My prayer to you young people: don't repeat the mistakes my generation made with technology." Using **AI/digital to automate internal bureaucracy** (approval apps, GPT-written reports, more meetings/pulse-surveys) makes complicatedness *easier* instead of managing complexity. **Use modern technology to manage complexity, not complicatedness.**
- **We're not more intelligent, work is more complex.** We aren't 6× more individually intelligent than 50 years ago, but work is 6× more complex — so the answer is **ways of working that better use collective intelligence**, i.e. **cooperation**, not new systems/structures.
- **Three multipliers:** cooperation, engagement, teamwork. Relay-team metaphor: individually slower, collectively fastest because they cooperate.
- **Roundabout vs traffic lights.** At a roundabout people must cooperate and use judgment (no one texts while navigating one); a signalised crossroads makes people obey markings. Organisations add "markings on the ground" (policies, process, signs) — complicating without managing complexity.
- **Engagement & mastery.** "Become one of the top five people in a domain on earth" — pick a domain, refine it; work breeds passion → engagement (even the hotel receptionist example). Air-traffic-controllers' work-to-rule (apply 100% of rules → nothing takes off) shows rules alone fail; judgment/engagement is essential.
- **Quality as compass; ambition as simplifier.** Define **quality** with your people (fit-for-purpose, durable) and use judgment; go for the *most ambitious* challenges — the solutions end up more valuable, safer, and ultimately **simpler**. Trains-on-time example: 80% of reporting is about delayed trains — so help put trains on time, and the reporting (and unhappy customers) shrink. Einstein: analyse complexity to find simplicity ("as simple as possible, but not simpler").
