# Strategic centering

**Strategic centering** (Rita McGrath, 2026) is **deliberately choosing one organizing principle** to guide resource allocation, opportunity selection, and organizational identity in a *dematerializing* world. It answers a single question — **"What are we really about?"** — and substitutes for the defensible-position logic of the inherited strategy frameworks once industry boundaries dissolve.

The premise is **dematerialization**: in 1975 ≈83% of Fortune 500 assets were tangible; today ≈90% of corporate value is **intangible** (software, data, brands, relationships, IP, capabilities). When value dematerializes, the old anchors — defensible position, stable industry, long-lived physical assets — lose their power, and "what industry are you in?" / "what is your competitive advantage?" stop being answerable. McGrath positions this against [[porter-five-forces|Porter's Five Forces]], the resource-based view (VRIO), and Blue Ocean as tools "forged in the stuff economy." Source: [[2026-07-01-mcgrath-2026-power-of-strategic-centering]].

## What centering is not

- **Not purpose** — too aspirational to decide between, e.g., experimental plays and beloved musicals.
- **Not vision** — a static destination rather than a dynamic direction.
- **Not core competence** — which "becomes a prison when the underlying technology shifts."

It is closer to Levitt's "what business are you really in?", but goes further: choosing the *dimension* along which the firm pursues a **coherent opportunity set** as industries themselves dissolve.

## What a center does

1. **Bounds the opportunity set without closing it** — you cannot pursue everything, but a center admits possibilities that share a common logic (Novartis's center opened radioligands, gene therapy, siRNA).
2. **Resolves capital-allocation dilemmas** — once the center is clear, resources flow toward it; competing-logic battles vanish. This is the [[dynamic-capabilities|seizing-side]] `balancing-digital-portfolios` move.
3. **Enables permissionless action** — when everyone shares the center, people act without waiting for approval; speed rises and the return on internal politics falls (the link to [[smart-simplicity]]: cooperation over structure).

## The five centers

| Center | Core question | Best when… | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Mission** | What problem keeps us up at night? | Problem large and enduring; addressing technologies in flux | Mission drift |
| **Customer** | Whose needs do we understand better than anyone? | Needs are complex, evolving, cut across industries | Conflict with other corporate goals |
| **Technology** | What capabilities transfer across domains? | Capabilities deep, long trajectory, multi-market | Technological narcissism |
| **National ecosystem** | What system are we essential to? | Scale/patience beyond private capital; geopolitics create value | Dependency; global uncompetitiveness |
| **Friction erasure** | What's still absurdly hard? | Legacy complexity, regulatory fragmentation, poor UX | Scope creep |

**Worked examples:** Mission — Novartis (innovative medicines), Shopify (commerce for everyone). Customer — Amazon (work backward from the customer), Airbnb (Chesky's "founder mode" recentering on the guest). Technology — Fujifilm (vs Kodak), Shell "molecule company" vs BP "integrated energy", Nvidia (parallel computing → AI). National ecosystem — TSMC (Taiwan's "silicon shield"), Samsung/Korea. Friction erasure — Toss, DBS ("world's best digital bank"), the Google/Apple/Facebook platform plays.

**Matching center to dematerialization level:** physical-output industries (construction, mining) → ecosystem or technology; partially dematerialized (healthcare, education) → mission or customer; digital-value-but-legacy-complexity (financial services, media) → friction erasure; already-digital (software, AI) → mission or customer (technology alone no longer differentiates).

## Leading a centered organization

McGrath's research surprise: effective leaders of centered firms are **not** hands-off servant-leaders but **deeply engaged, highly visible** presences who *maintain the center* — sensing weak signals (Narasimhan and radioligands), giving direction without dictating method. Centering is the **prerequisite for permissionlessness**: without a clear center, removing hierarchy produces confusion; with one, it produces speed.

## Relation to the rest of the wiki

- **Against positioning.** Strategic centering is a direct critique of [[porter-five-forces]] and the resource-based view — it argues their stable-industry assumptions fail under dematerialization. It extends McGrath's transient-advantage thesis (durable positions don't exist; hold a center, not a position).
- **With dynamic capabilities.** Where [[dynamic-capabilities]] / [[warner-wager-process-model]] give the *machinery* of sensing/seizing/transforming, centering supplies the *criterion* — what to sense for and seize. "Leaders maintaining the center" is sensing made concrete.
- **With ambidexterity.** A center bounds the explore portfolio of [[organizational-ambidexterity]] so experimentation stays coherent; it is the discipline that stops explore-mode from sprawling.
- **With the wiki's integrative model.** In [[cmr-framework]] a "center" is the single organizing principle a firm's capability engine is tuned to, and McGrath's weak-signal sensing maps onto that model's **Sense** phase.
- **With options thinking.** "Permissionless action" and committing to one center while shedding the rest echoes the reversibility logic of [[two-way-door-decisions]] and [[real-options]] — and the model-over-position view of [[business-model-innovation]].

## Debates and supersession

- **Single, recent, non-peer-reviewed source.** The concept rests on one HBR practitioner article ([[2026-07-01-mcgrath-2026-power-of-strategic-centering]]); confidence is held at 0.7. The empirical backing is illustrative cases (Novartis, Fujifilm, TSMC, Toss), not systematic evidence — selection-on-the-dependent-variable risk (we hear about centered winners, not centered failures).
- **Open contradiction with [[porter-five-forces]].** McGrath frames Five Forces as obsolete for dematerialized industries; Porter's defenders would reply that industry structure still governs profitability even when boundaries blur. The wiki holds both: Five Forces for stable-boundary contexts, centering for dissolving-boundary ones — the unresolved question is where the boundary between those regimes actually falls.
- **Open question.** Is "friction erasure" a genuinely distinct center or a tactic available under any of the other four? McGrath lists it as co-equal; it reads as the most porous category.
