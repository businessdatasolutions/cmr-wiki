# Two-way door decisions (reversibility heuristic)

The **two-way door** heuristic, popularised by Jeff Bezos (Amazon's 1997 shareholder letter; reprised on the Lex Fridman podcast and summarised in [[2025-01-19-bezos-two-way-door-decision-making]]), classifies decisions by **reversibility**:

- **Two-way door (reversible, Type 2):** if it goes wrong you can walk back through and pick another door. Low risk. → **Decide fast, delegate, optimise for speed and learning.**
- **One-way door (irreversible, Type 1):** once through, there is no coming back. High consequence. → **Slow down, gather data, consult, deliberate.**

The dominant error is treating **reversible** decisions as if they were irreversible — over-analysing low-stakes choices, which kills organisational pace. The discipline is to **match deliberation to irreversibility**, not to decision visibility or emotional weight.

## The decision rule

| | **Two-way door** | **One-way door** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Reversibility** | Easy to reverse | Hard/impossible to reverse |
| **Examples** | Test a feature, pilot an ad, trial a supplier | Acquisition, major rebrand, binding long-term contract |
| **Speed** | Fast — set a timeline / delegate | Slow — structured, deliberate |
| **Optimise for** | Learning, experimentation | Thoroughness, risk analysis |

**Application recipe:** (1) categorise — "if this fails, can I easily reverse it?"; (2) pace two-way doors — reduce analysis, delegate, focus on learning; (3) steady for one-way doors — gather data, consult, but don't let fear paralyse; (4) reflect & iterate on outcomes regardless of type.

## Why it is an option in disguise

A two-way door is the managerial framing of an **[[real-options|option to abandon/reverse]]**: when the cost of reversal is low, the option to back out is cheap and valuable, so the rational move is to **acquire and exercise it cheaply** — i.e. experiment. Damodaran's abandonment option ([[2008-01-01-damodaran-2008-strategic-risk-taking-ch8-real-options]]) is the quantitative version: reversibility *creates* option value, and high option value justifies acting under uncertainty rather than waiting for certainty. The Bezos heuristic is the boardroom shorthand for the same maths.
