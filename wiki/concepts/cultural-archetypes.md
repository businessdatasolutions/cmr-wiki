# Cultural archetypes (performance / people / customer)

**Cultural archetypes** are a parsimonious three-factor structure of organizational culture introduced by [[2024-06-20-oreilly-2024-culture-archetypes-firm-performance|O'Reilly, Cao & Sull (2024)]]. Factor-analysing the [[big-9-cultural-values|nine Big 9 values]] (varimax rotation, n = 309 firms) yields **three underlying archetypes** that integrate the "proliferation of constructs" plaguing culture research:

| Archetype | Big-9 values that load on it | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Performance-orientation** | Execution (0.87), Agility (0.87), Innovation (0.70), Performance (0.63), Collaboration (0.54) | External adaptation + drive results |
| **People** | Integrity (0.82), Respect (0.71), Diversity (0.65), Collaboration (0.59) | Internal coordination + common identity |
| **Customer** | Customer (0.89) | External focus on customers |

(Loadings from [[oreilly-2024-archetype-factor-loadings]]; Collaboration cross-loads on Performance and People.)

## The load-bearing finding: only performance-orientation pays

Regressing objective firm performance on the three archetypes (controlling industry, size, CEO tenure — see [[oreilly-2024-archetype-performance-regressions]]):

- **Performance-orientation** → **positive** on Tobin's Q, ROE, revenue growth, analyst buys. The archetype that moves financials.
- **People** → **no significant association with any objective performance measure** — despite the prior literature tying people-oriented cultures to satisfaction and engagement.
- **Customer** → **negative** on analyst buy recommendations (analysts may read customer-focus as less short-term-financial-focused); otherwise null.

This separates **employee-experience outcomes** (where the people archetype / respect dominate — see [[sull-2021-ten-culture-elements]]) from **objective firm-performance outcomes** (where performance-orientation dominates). It offers a clean explanation for why decades of culture→performance research produced "mixed and confusing" results: studies measuring people-oriented culture against objective metrics would find nothing.

## How the archetypes integrate prior frameworks

The paper argues the three map onto established culture instruments:

- **Competing Values Framework** (Cameron & Quinn) — external vs internal focus.
- **Denison DOCS** — mission/involvement (external/internal orientations).
- **Hartnell et al. metathemes** — task-oriented (→ performance) and relationship-oriented (→ people).
- **Cultural-evolution theory** — external adaptation (performance + customer) and internal adjustment (people).

## Relation to the rest of the wiki

- **Performance-orientation ↔ dynamic capabilities.** Agility and innovation load on this archetype, linking it to [[dynamic-capabilities]] (digital-seizing/strategic-agility) and [[business-model-innovation]].
- **People archetype ↔ employee experience.** The people values (respect, integrity, diversity) are where the [[2021-09-16-ten-things-corporate-culture|10 employee-rated elements]] concentrate — but for *experience*, not *financials*.
- **Culture as risk.** The archetypes give a structured way to read culture as a firm-value variable, connecting to [[strategic-risk-management]].

## Debates and supersession

_Single-source concept (one peer-reviewed paper)._ Key open questions carried from the source: **causality** (the design is cross-sectional/associational); whether the **people-archetype null** reflects true absence of effect or measurement ceilings in Glassdoor data; **generalisability** beyond large U.S. public firms; and **single-pipeline dependency** (the culture measure is the Sull/CultureX one, and Sull co-authored the paper). A second, independent archetype study would lift confidence above 0.8.
