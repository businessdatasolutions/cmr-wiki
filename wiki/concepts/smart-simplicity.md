# Smart Simplicity (complexity vs complicatedness)

**Smart Simplicity** is Yves Morieux's (BCG) approach to organisational performance, built on a sharp distinction:

- **Complexity** — the genuine, rising difficulty of the business environment (more markets, products, stakeholders, requirements). Real and unavoidable; the *economic complexity* of economies and firms keeps increasing.
- **Complicatedness** — the *self-inflicted* organisational response to complexity: more committees, layers, silos, KPIs, processes, coordination bodies, and reports. Complicatedness is what destroys productivity.

The core claim ([[2024-02-02-bcg-yves-morieux-complexity-complicatedness|Morieux's Brain Bar 2023 talk]]): business challenges are ~**6× more complex** than mid-20th-century, organisations have become ~**35× more complicated**, and productivity growth has fallen to its lowest of the century *despite* abundant innovation (cloud, big data, AI). Organisations "respond to complexity with complicatedness" — and the productivity potential of innovation disappears into internal coordination.

## The diagnosis

- **Reporting substitutes for trust.** "I ask you to write a report on the action I don't trust you to have taken — why should I trust the report?" Coordination and reporting consume manager time; people disengage.
- **The light-bulb parable.** ~Seven approvals to change a light bulb (facility/safety/buyer/category/compliance roles) — complicatedness made concrete.
- **The AI trap.** The worst use of digital/AI is to *automate the internal bureaucracy* (approval apps, GPT-written reports, more meetings/pulse-surveys), making complicatedness easier rather than managing complexity. **Use modern technology to manage complexity, not complicatedness.**

## The prescription — cooperation over structure

We are not individually more intelligent than 50 years ago, but work is far more complex; so the answer is **ways of working that better use collective intelligence** — **cooperation**, not new systems or structures. Three multipliers: **cooperation, engagement, teamwork**.

- **Roundabout vs traffic lights.** At a roundabout people must cooperate and use judgment (no one texts while navigating one); a signalised crossroads makes people obey markings. Organisations add "markings on the ground" (policies, process, signs) — complicating without managing complexity.
- **Engagement & mastery.** "Become one of the top five people in a domain on earth"; work breeds passion → engagement. Air-traffic-controllers' work-to-rule (apply 100% of rules → nothing moves) shows rules without judgment fail.
- **Quality as compass; ambition as simplifier.** Define quality (fit-for-purpose, durable) with your people and use judgment; pursue the *most ambitious* challenges — the solutions end up more valuable, safer, and ultimately **simpler** (trains-on-time beats reporting-on-late-trains). Einstein: "as simple as possible, but not simpler."

## The Six Simple Rules (from the book — citation to chase)

Morieux's fuller framework is articulated as **six simple rules** in **Morieux & Tollman (2014), *Six Simple Rules: How to Manage Complexity without Getting Complicated*** (HBR Press). The book is **not yet in the wiki** and the rules are **not cleanly enumerated in the ingested talk**, so the following is a forward-pointer to be verified on acquisition, not a claim sourced from the video:

1. Understand what your people actually do.
2. Reinforce integrators.
3. Increase the total quantity of power.
4. Increase reciprocity.
5. Extend the shadow of the future.
6. Reward those who cooperate.

- **vs culture.** Cooperation + engagement are the people-side levers in [[organizational-culture]] and echo [[devops|DevOps]] culture (autonomy, trust, removing approval friction; MTTR-style judgment over rule-compliance).
- **vs dynamic capabilities.** Complicatedness maps to the W&W **internal barriers** (rigid planning, hierarchy) and cooperation to **renewal of the collaborative approach** ([[warner-wager-process-model]], [[dynamic-capabilities]]).

## Debates and supersession

_Single-source concept, and an ASR-degraded one._ The primary articulation — **Morieux & Tollman (2014)** — would substantiate the six rules and raise confidence above 0.75. Open tension worth noting: Morieux's "cooperation over structure/process" is in productive friction with [[devops]]/[[devsecops]]' embrace of *process automation and guardrails* — the reconciliation is that both target the *same enemy* (coordination friction / hand-offs), one via human cooperation, the other via automation that removes the need for it.
