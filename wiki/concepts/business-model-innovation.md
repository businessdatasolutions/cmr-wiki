# Business model innovation

**Business model innovation (BMI)** is the discipline of creating competitive advantage by changing **how a firm creates, delivers, and captures value** — the *business model* — rather than by inventing a new product or technology. The wiki's anchor is Strategyzer's body of work (Osterwalder & Pigneur, *The Invincible Company*): the sharp claim is that **business-model innovation, not R&D or technology, is the larger and less-exploited source of value**.

> "Business model innovation is the biggest unexplored possibility for large and small companies because very few people master it." — Alex Osterwalder, via [[2024-11-29-sme-innovation-powerhouse]]

## The core claim: model over technology

[[2024-11-29-sme-innovation-powerhouse]] reports PwC's *Innovation 1000* finding of a **consistent ~zero correlation between R&D spend and returns** over ~two decades: two firms investing equally can see +50% vs −50% outcomes. "Having a cool technology is not the same as having a value proposition." Value comes from **desirability + viability + a model that monetises** — and for SMEs, BMI is "finding the right business model" for an existing product/service, not groundbreaking invention.

Failure/success contrast: **Quibi** burned $1.75B and failed by not distinguishing Explore from Exploit; **Laurastar** succeeded by exploring three business models in parallel at low cost and scaling the viable one (*Iggy*) — a **business-model shift**, not a budget.

## The Portfolio Map lifecycle

[[2019-05-19-business-model-evolution-portfolio-map]] plots business activities on a **risk-vs-return** field split into an **Explore portfolio** and an **Exploit portfolio**, with a lifecycle that runs through them:

**Explore (search / iterative):**
1. **Ideation** — new value-proposition/model ideas.
2. **Discovery** — first desirability/viability evidence ("is there something here?").
3. **Validation** — strong desirability/viability + first feasibility; technical MVP ("can we build this?").
4. **Confirmation** — proceed or pivot; invest in acquisition/marketing ("can we do this at scale?").

**Transfer** — hand-off from Explore to Exploit.

**Exploit (existing business):**
1. **Transfer / Launch** (customer focus)
2. **Sustaining Innovation** (market/scale focus)
3. **Efficiency** (shareholder focus)
4. **Mature Business**
5. **Declining Business**
6. **Renovation** — shift the model before the business dies.

**Worked Renovation case — Hilti:** product manufacturer → "tools as a service" reframe after the 2013 crisis and low-cost competition → 2018 net sales CHF 5.7B. The canonical product→service model shift.

## Relation to the rest of the wiki

- **Ambidexterity.** BMI is the work of the **Explore portfolio**; [[organizational-ambidexterity]] is the firm-level capacity to run Explore alongside Exploit.
- **Options & parallel bets.** Running multiple cheap, reversible explorations (Laurastar's three teams) is portfolio-of-[[real-options|options]] thinking and the [[two-way-door-decisions]] reversibility logic applied to model search.
- **Dynamic capabilities.** BMI maps onto the W&W `strategic-renewal/business-model` outcome and the `rapid-prototyping` / `balancing-digital-portfolios` seizing microfoundations ([[warner-wager-process-model]], [[dynamic-capabilities]]).
- **Structure side.** Where [[porter-five-forces]] analyses industry structure, BMI is a lever to *reshape* a firm's position within (or escape) that structure by changing the model.

## Debates and supersession

Both sources are **Strategyzer practitioner material** — mutually reinforcing but not independent, and built on the same *Invincible Company* framework. The headline empirical claim (PwC *Innovation 1000* zero R&D-returns correlation) is **reported second-hand**; acquire the primary PwC/Booz reports before citing the figure as established. Independent BMI scholarship (Chesbrough, Zott & Amit, Teece's "business models and dynamic capabilities") would broaden and partly contest the Strategyzer framing. Confidence held at 0.75 pending an independent source.
