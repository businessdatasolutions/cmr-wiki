# Organizational culture

**Organizational (corporate) culture** is, in the wiki's working definition (Sull & Sull, via the [[2019-06-26-the-9-key-values-for-company-culture|Culture 500]]):

> a set of values that are deeply held, widely shared, and shape behavior on a day-to-day basis.

It is the **norms-and-values layer** of Schein's (2010) three-layer model (underlying assumptions → norms & values → visible artifacts) — the layer most studies operationalise, and the one the wiki's culture cluster measures.

## Why culture matters (and to whom)

Culture is increasingly treated as a **financial** variable, not an HR soft topic. In a Wharton survey cited in the [[2023-05-31-building-a-winning-workplace-culture|MIT SMR webinar]], **>90% of CEOs and CFOs** (not CHROs) said good culture increases firm value, and a near-identical share said toxic culture leads to unethical behaviour. Toxic culture was the **single best predictor of attrition** in the early Great Resignation — ~10× more powerful than compensation.

## The measurement problem

Culture has been "notoriously difficult to measure in a scientific way." Two traps:

1. **Official core values are unreliable.** ~80% of large firms publish 3–5 core values, but there is **~zero correlation** between the values a firm emphasises and how well employees say it lives them. (Wells Fargo listed exemplary values the year before its fraud — the canonical "poster on the wall" cautionary tale.)
2. **Surveys/qualitative methods don't scale** to the cross-organizational samples needed to link culture to objective outcomes.

The wiki's cluster resolves both via [[measuring-culture-with-nlp|NLP measurement of employee reviews]] — see that concept for the method.

## The structure of culture in this wiki

Three nested constructs, increasingly compressed:

1. **The [[big-9-cultural-values|Big 9 cultural values]]** — the nine values most cited by firms *and* most impactful on results (Agility, Collaboration, Customer, Diversity, Execution, Innovation, Integrity, Performance, Respect).
2. **The [[cultural-archetypes|three cultural archetypes]]** — performance-orientation, people, customer — into which the nine values factor-analyse ([[2024-06-20-oreilly-2024-culture-archetypes-firm-performance|O'Reilly, Cao & Sull 2024]]).
3. **The two dependent variables** culture is measured *against*:
   - **Employee experience** — what predicts how employees *rate* their culture: respect dominates ([[2021-09-16-ten-things-corporate-culture|the 10 elements]], Respect ~18×).
   - **Objective firm performance** — what predicts financials: the performance-orientation archetype dominates; the people archetype shows *no* objective-performance association.

The crucial wiki claim: **these two dependent variables are different.** People/respect drive how it *feels* to work somewhere; performance-orientation drives the *financials*. Conflating them is the source of much of the literature's "ambiguous" culture→performance evidence.

## Debates and supersession

- **Employee-experience vs firm-performance drivers.** Respect/people dominate employee culture ratings ([[2021-09-16-ten-things-corporate-culture]]) but the **people archetype shows no objective-firm-performance association** ([[2024-06-20-oreilly-2024-culture-archetypes-firm-performance]]). Not a contradiction — different dependent variables — but a tension the wiki flags explicitly.
- **Source independence.** Six of the seven sources come from the **Sull / CultureX / Culture 500** programme; they corroborate each other but are **not independent**. The lone external-leaning anchor is the peer-reviewed JBR paper (which still uses the Sull measure and has Sull as a co-author). Confidence held at 0.85 pending a genuinely independent culture-measurement source.
- **Open questions:** causality (culture↔performance is cross-sectional/associational); whether the people-archetype null reflects measurement (Glassdoor positivity ceilings) or a true absence of effect; cross-cultural / non-U.S. generalisability.
